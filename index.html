When conversations start to take more effort

Changes in hearing can be subtle and easy to explain away. You might recognize some of these moments:

  • Missing words

    People seem to mumble, and you find yourself asking them to repeat what they said.

  • Struggling with background noise

    Busy rooms, restaurants or meetings make it harder to follow the voice you’re trying to hear.

  • Turning up the TV

    You set the volume higher than others in the room find comfortable.

These experiences don’t automatically mean you have hearing loss or need hearing aids. A hearing test gives you a clear picture, and it can detect changes often before you notice them yourself.

Understand your hearing before choosing anything

Your first visit is about learning how you hear. Any recommendation comes after that, and only if it suits your results.

  1. 1

    Your hearing test

    In a quiet, confidential room, you listen through headphones to tones and speech while your responses are measured and recorded.

    Recommendations are based on how you actually hear.

  2. 2

    Your results, explained

    We review your results with you on an audiogram, a simple chart showing how loud sounds need to be for you to hear them at different pitches.

    You understand what’s happening before deciding anything.

  3. 3

    Options that suit you

    If hearing aids are appropriate, we guide you toward styles and technology that fit your hearing needs, lifestyle and budget.

    Choices that make sense for your daily life.

  4. 4

    Fitting, learning and support

    Devices are fitted and set for you, with custom ear impressions when needed, plus help with care, charging, phone connectivity and features. Follow-ups and adjustments continue as your needs change.

    More confidence using your hearing aids day to day.

Hearing aids aren’t the answer for every type of hearing loss. Some causes, such as a blockage or fluid in the ear, may need medical treatment instead, and your results help show which path makes sense.

Which tests might be included?
Pure-tone audiometry
You hear beeps at different volumes and pitches through headphones, which identifies the quietest sounds you can hear across frequencies.
Tympanometry
Gently changes the air pressure in your ear canal to check how your middle ear is working. It can show earwax buildup or fluid behind the eardrum.
Otoacoustic emissions (OAE)
A tiny probe measures the faint sounds produced by healthy hair cells in the inner ear.

The tests used depend on your situation, so not every visit includes all three.

Hearing aids fitted to your everyday life

The right hearing aid depends on your hearing profile, how you spend your days and your budget. With eight manufacturers to choose from, there’s room to find options that fit you.

Discreet styles, where they suit your hearing

Styles range from behind-the-ear and sleek receiver-in-canal models to custom designs that sit in the ear canal. Some of the smallest are virtually invisible.

The smallest styles are generally suited to mild to moderate hearing loss, so your results guide which options are realistic for you.

A fit and sound set for you

Custom ear impressions are taken when needed for a snug, comfortable fit, and settings are adjusted to your hearing so sound is balanced and clear. A comfortable fit can make it easier to wear your hearing aids for more of the day.

Help with your phone and everyday technology

Most hearing aids are Bluetooth-enabled and can stream calls and music from compatible smartphones. We set up streaming and environment presets and guide you through pairing at your fitting, so the features are ready to use.

Connectivity varies by model and phone.

Hearing aid styles at a glance
Behind-the-ear (BTE)
Easy to handle, and most include wireless features. Typically used for moderate to severe hearing loss.
Receiver-in-canal (RIC)
A sleek profile with wireless connectivity as standard. Typically used for moderate to severe hearing loss.
In-the-ear (ITE)
Custom-made half- or full-shell designs with longer battery life and manual controls. Used for mild to severe hearing loss.
In-the-canal (ITC, CIC, IIC)
Smaller custom styles. CIC and IIC models are virtually invisible and suited to mild to moderate hearing loss.

Which style suits you depends on the degree of your hearing loss and your preferences.

Common questions

How do I know if I need hearing aids?

If you often ask people to repeat themselves, turn the TV volume up, or feel that people are mumbling, it’s a good idea to have a hearing evaluation. A comprehensive test shows the type and extent of any hearing loss, which guides what we recommend.

Will a hearing test mean I need hearing aids?

Not necessarily. The test helps determine the type and severity of any hearing loss and whether hearing aids are the right solution for you.

Some types of hearing loss, such as those caused by earwax or fluid in the middle ear, may be treated medically rather than with hearing aids.

Will people be able to see my hearing aids?

Many modern hearing aids are small and discreet. Receiver-in-canal models have a sleek profile, and completely-in-canal (CIC) and invisible-in-canal (IIC) styles are virtually invisible.

The smallest styles are generally suited to mild to moderate hearing loss, so the options open to you depend on your hearing test results.

Will my insurance cover hearing aids?

It depends on your plan. The best way to be sure is to call us. We work with many insurance providers and will spend the time on hold to see if you have any hearing aid benefits, so you don’t have to.

We also accept all major credit cards and CareCredit®, and offer financing plans.

Can my hearing aids connect to my phone?

Most hearing aids are Bluetooth-enabled and can connect directly to smartphones, so you can stream calls, music and more. We can guide you through the pairing process during your fitting. Compatibility depends on the hearing aid model and your phone.

What support is available after I get hearing aids?

Support continues after your fitting:

  • Follow-up visits and adjustments as your hearing needs change.
  • Guidance on cleaning, storage and an annual maintenance appointment. With proper care, hearing aids typically last 5–7 years.
  • Repairs, many handled in our office, including hearing aids purchased somewhere else.
