Recognize any of these listening difficulties?

Changes in hearing can show up in ordinary moments.

  • Missing words

    People seem to mumble, and you find yourself asking them to repeat what they said.

  • Following conversations in noise

    Busy rooms, restaurants or meetings make it harder to follow the voice you’re trying to hear.

  • Turning up the TV

    You set the volume higher than others in the room find comfortable.

Here for someone you love? The same first step can help them understand their hearing.

These experiences don’t automatically mean you have hearing loss or need hearing aids. A hearing test can identify changes you haven’t noticed and help guide your next steps.

Which tests might be included?Expand to see the tests and what each one checks.
Pure-tone audiometry
You hear beeps at different volumes and pitches through headphones, which identifies the quietest sounds you can hear across frequencies.
Tympanometry
Measures how your eardrum and middle ear respond to changes in air pressure. Results can help identify middle-ear problems, such as fluid behind the eardrum.
Otoacoustic emissions (OAE)
A tiny probe measures the faint sounds produced by healthy hair cells in the inner ear.

The tests used depend on your situation, so not every visit includes all three.

Compare hearing aid stylesExplore behind-the-ear and in-ear options. Expand the guide to see how they differ.
Behind-the-ear (BTE)
Worn behind the ear, with sound delivered through an earpiece or tube. Models can suit mild to profound hearing loss.
Receiver-in-canal (RIC)
A small device behind the ear connects by a thin wire to a speaker in the ear canal. Suitability and wireless features depend on the model.
In-the-ear (ITE)
Custom half- or full-shell designs in the outer ear that can suit mild to severe hearing loss. Controls, battery life and connectivity vary by model.
In-the-canal (ITC, CIC, IIC)
Small custom designs that sit partly or deeply in the canal. Visibility and suitability depend on your hearing and the model.

Which style suits you depends on the degree of your hearing loss and your preferences.

Questions before your visit?

Find answers about hearing tests, hearing aids and ongoing support. For questions about your own needs, contact our Sherman team.

When should I book a hearing test?

If you often miss words, ask for repetition or turn up the TV, a hearing test can help you understand what’s happening and guide the next step.

Does a hearing test mean I need hearing aids?

Not necessarily. Your results help determine whether hearing aids are appropriate. Some causes of hearing loss, such as earwax or fluid in the middle ear, may need medical treatment instead.

Will people notice my hearing aids?

Some designs are discreet or virtually invisible. Which styles suit you depends on your hearing results and the model. See the hearing aid style guide for examples.

Will my insurance cover hearing aids?

Coverage depends on your plan. Our team works with many insurance providers and can contact your insurer to check for hearing aid benefits.

For other ways to pay, see our payment options.

Can hearing aids connect to my phone?

Many models stream calls and music from compatible smartphones. We can help with pairing at your fitting; compatibility depends on the model and phone.

What support is available after fitting?

Support continues after your fitting:

  • Follow-up visits and adjustments as your needs change.
  • Guidance on cleaning and storage, plus an annual maintenance appointment.
  • Repairs, many handled in our office, including hearing aids purchased elsewhere.

With proper care, hearing aids typically last 5–7 years.

Who will I see at my appointment?

HearCare is owned and led by Kenneth M. Sheppard, Jr. and Jill A. Sheppard, both Licensed Fitters and Dispensers of Hearing Instruments.

Ask when you call or text to confirm which team member you’ll see at the Sherman office.

How should I prepare for my hearing test?

Note the situations where hearing is difficult and be ready to discuss your medical history and current medications. You’re welcome to bring a friend or family member for support.
